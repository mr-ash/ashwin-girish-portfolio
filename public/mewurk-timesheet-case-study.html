Project goal

Time tracking had become a challenge — scattered entries, delayed approvals, and recurring payroll errors. With Mewurk Timesheet, we set out to simplify the process: giving employees an effortless way to log hours, managers real-time visibility, and HR the accuracy and compliance they needed.

Problem statements

01

Inaccurate time logging

Problem: Employees often forget to record their work hours during the day, leading to missing or overlapping entries — and inaccurate availability data.

Impact: Payroll errors and unreliable project data downstream.

02

Manual data entry & verification

Problem: Timesheets required manual entry and line-by-line verification by HR, making the process slow and error-prone.

Impact: Hours of repetitive HR effort every cycle, with mistakes still slipping through.

03

Limited real-time visibility

Problem: Managers and HR lacked real-time insight into hours logged and project allocation — relying on end-of-week or end-of-month summaries.

Impact: Overloaded team members and capacity problems surfaced too late to act on.

04

Inefficient approval workflows

Problem: Managers and HR reviewed and approved timesheets manually, one by one, with unclear multi-level workflows.

Impact: Approval bottlenecks that delayed payroll and frustrated everyone in the chain.

User research

We conducted user interviews with HR managers and employees from diverse industries — including IT, manufacturing and healthcare — to understand how timesheet functions across various sectors.

70%

of employees admitted to forgetting to log hours on time, causing submission delays.

60%

of managers found tracking and approving timesheets manually to be time-consuming.

55%

of HR professionals reported frequent payroll discrepancies due to inaccurate timesheets.

45%

of employees said existing timesheet tools feel confusing or cumbersome to use.

User interviews

We conducted in-depth interviews with 8 participants — including HR professionals, project managers, and employees — to uncover how they currently track work hours and where the existing process breaks down.

Takeaway 01Manual effort and data inaccuracy are major pain points across all roles.
Takeaway 02All users want automation, clarity, and visibility in the timesheet workflow.
Takeaway 03HR strongly emphasised integration with project management for seamless resource tracking.

Did we solve the right problems, the right way?

Usability testing — task scenarios
  • Submit a timesheet for the current week with 2 projects assigned.
  • Add a new activity that doesn't exist in the system during time entry.
  • Approve a direct reportee's timesheet from the manager dashboard.
  • Find and fix an overlapping time entry flagged by the system.
  • Configure automated reminder settings as an HR admin.
High severity

Time log vs. timesheet confusion

Participants conflated "time logs" and "timesheets" — the same label was used for both, breaking their mental model of the flow.

Medium severity

Overlap warnings dismissed too easily

Users skimmed past passive overlap warnings and submitted anyway — errors continued into the approval stage.

Low severity

Quick add discoverability

Some users didn't notice the quick-add option during time entry and abandoned the task to look for an admin.

Iterations after testing

Before
After
Same label used for both "time logs" and "timesheets".
Navigation split into "My time logs" and "My timesheets" with distinct icons and copy.
Overlap warning was a passive inline banner users scrolled past.
Blocking modal — the user must resolve the conflict before proceeding to submit.
Quick add hidden as a small unlabelled icon inside the activity dropdown.
Prominent "+ Add new" action with a label, surfaced directly in the entry flow.
Approval status visible only in the manager's section.
Status badge added to the employee's timesheet view after submission.
